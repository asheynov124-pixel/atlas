## Grammar: Stationery suite (new, defined here)

The page is a wedding stationery suite laid on a table. The unit is a **card**
(invitation, story enclosure, the evening card, details card, reply card), not
an act or a chapter.

- **Nav:** a suite index in the margin, one entry per card, which jumps.
- **Hero:** the invitation, sealed in its lined envelope; scroll opens it.
- **Sequence:** cards in the order a guest reads a suite. One paper family for
  every ground; one photographic full-frame moment, reserved for the peak.
- **Close:** the reply card. Small type, a held countdown, the calendar action
  set as a printed line, not a button island.
- **Bans:** scrub video; dark grounds (the peak's photograph is the only full
  frame); kinetic character splitting; magnetic CTA; spotlight; invented
  numbers; scroll cues; section counters.

Why not the eight: filmic one-shot implies one carried argument and hides the
schedule; chaptered editorial forbids media above the fold and the envelope is
the whole first impression; live surface / continuous world / split stage /
rhythmic cutlist have nothing to operate, travel, compare or pulse; typographic
poster throws away their photographs; gallery treats the couple as a
catalogue. The suite is what a guest already knows how to read.
