A Wikipedia app for one reader

Lens

Adi's app for reading Wikipedia like a magazine. This page is Lens itself, running on a saved copy of Wikipedia.

Every morning

It opens on today.

The featured article, what the world is reading, and this day in history. On 20 July 2026, a curlew sandpiper had the cover.

Most read today

Real readers, counted.

That day, 1,200,352 people read about Lamine Yamal. Further down, 20 July 1969: two men walk on the Moon.

Search

Spell it wrong.

Type “girl with a perl earing”. Photos arrive while you type, until the spelling goes wrong.

Wikipedia shrugs. Lens asks.

Wikipedia's own results are a list of Dawson's Creek episodes and an FBI spy. Lens notices, and asks: Did you mean girl with a pearl earring?

It only asks when it's unsure: fewer than 100 matches, and no page with that exact name.

Then the right one, big.

The best match gets a card. Every other result shows its photo, the words that matched, and how long it takes to read.

How it was made

Built by a relay of four AIs.

  1. Claude builds itA working version that meets every check.
  2. Gemini designs itType, colour, spacing, touch targets, reading comfort.
  3. ChatGPT audits itBugs, lost-data risks, edge cases.
  4. Grok tries to break itAllowed to change no code. Only to report.
  5. Claude closes itFixes what Grok found, checks nothing was lost, publishes.

Grok reported “Changed: nothing.” Its file came back about 10 KB shorter and would have stopped articles from loading. Stage 5 started from ChatGPT's file instead.

Since then: video in every article, a Home that keeps going, Reels, Story, the Trail, a paper theme and a smarter Search.
